# Screening: Two Lines to Your Past Self

Brief order: (1) line 1 warns about one specific moment, line 2 says why it
mattered; (2) the moment is concrete enough to picture; (3) line 2 traces real
cause and effect, not a restatement; (4) the two lines work as one, no wasted
words; (5) voice sounds written to one specific person, plain and vivid.

Scores: strong / ok / weak per criterion, in brief order.

## Candidates

### 1. The deleted message
L1: Don't delete the message you typed at 2 a.m. to the girl from the night bus.
L2: You never send another one, and the version of you who talks first never gets born.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong (2 a.m., night bus), C3 ok (a version of you never
getting born is poetic, not a traced consequence), C4 strong, C5 strong.
Verdict: line 2 drifts into metaphor instead of a concrete change.

### 2. The penalty
L1: When the coach asks who wants to take the penalty, raise your hand.
L2: You spend the next ten years watching other people take the shots you practiced for.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 strong, C4 strong, C5 strong.
Verdict: solid, but "watching other people take the shots" is a familiar
sports-regret shape; the consequence is passive.

### 3. The dinner table
L1: Don't laugh along when your friends call your brother weird at the dinner table.
L2: He stops talking to you for three years, and you spend the next ten learning how to earn it back.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 strong, C4 ok ("learning how to earn it back"
is vague work), C5 strong.
Verdict: strong consequence, but line 2's second half softens the landing.

### 4. The flat above the bakery
L1: Don't sign the lease on the flat above the bakery because it's cheap.
L2: The smell of bread at 4 a.m. ruins your sleep for a year, and you quit the job you moved there for.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 strong, C4 strong, C5 strong.
Verdict: vivid and funny, but the consequence (quitting a job over bread smell)
reads as a joke first and a warning second; the twist outshines the moment.

### 5. Stay for dinner (WINNER)
L1: When Dad says 'stay for dinner' on the last Sunday in March, stay.
L2: He dies in May, and that meal is the last time the whole family laughs in one room.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong (a spoken sentence, a named day), C3 strong
(death in May, last shared laugh: the moment's cost is named, not repeated),
C4 strong, C5 strong (plain, addressed to one person, no cleverness).
Verdict: wins on criterion order. It beats candidate 2 at C3 because the
consequence is a named, irreversible change rather than a passive decade of
watching. It beats candidate 8 at C5 because it stays plain where 8 gets
clever. Every word earns its place.

### 6. The ticket
L1: Don't throw the ticket away after the band finishes their first song.
L2: You miss the encore where they play the song that becomes your wedding song.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 ok (a missed song is a small stake for a
warning), C4 strong, C5 ok (reads like a cute story, not a warning to one person).
Verdict: stakes too low; the consequence charms instead of landing.

### 7. The kitchen table
L1: When she says 'tell me the truth' at the kitchen table, tell her.
L2: The lie costs you the marriage; the truth would only have cost you a weekend.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 strong, C4 strong, C5 ok (the parallel
"costs you / would only have cost you" is clever for its own sake).
Verdict: second place. Loses to 5 at C5: the twist is the point here, where in
5 the moment is the point.

### 8. The car with Mark
L1: Don't get in the car with Mark after the third drink at the wedding.
L2: The crash breaks your leg and your season, and you never play semi-pro again.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 strong, C4 strong, C5 strong.
Verdict: strong all round, but "the crash" does the work off-screen; the
warning names a risk, not a moment the reader can feel. Loses to 5 at C2:
a spoken invitation beats a counted drink.

### 9. See the teacher
L1: When the teacher hands back the essay with the red 'see me', go see her.
L2: She offers you the scholarship interview, and you spend the next decade paying rent instead of writing.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 ok ("paying rent instead of writing" is a
consequence, but the link from a missed interview to a decade of rent is
assumed, not traced), C4 ok, C5 strong.
Verdict: line 2 jumps a decade in one step; the cause and effect has a gap.

### 10. The job in the lift
L1: Don't say yes to the job in the lift because the salary sounds big.
L2: You spend four years counting down to Fridays and lose the habit of wanting anything.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 ok (a lift is a place, but "because the salary sounds
big" is a motive, not a moment), C3 ok, C4 strong, C5 strong.
Verdict: "lose the habit of wanting anything" is a good line, but the moment
itself is thin.

### 11. The hospital car park
L1: When your sister calls from the hospital car park at midnight, pick up.
L2: She needed one person to say 'come home', and she never asked anyone twice.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 ok (the consequence is implied, not shown:
what happened because she never asked again?), C4 strong, C5 strong.
Verdict: line 2 withholds the actual change; the reader has to fill it in.

### 12. The interview lie
L1: Don't say 'I'm fine' when your mum asks how the interview went on Tuesday night.
L2: That lie lets you stop trying for a year, and the job you wanted goes to someone who bombed it louder than you would have.
Scores: C1 strong, C2 strong, C3 strong, C4 ok (line 2 runs long, "louder than
you would have" is a second joke), C5 strong.
Verdict: good, but line 2 carries two ideas; candidate 5 lands the same shape
in fewer words.

## Winner

Candidate 5, "Stay for dinner":

> When Dad says 'stay for dinner' on the last Sunday in March, stay.
> He dies in May, and that meal is the last time the whole family laughs in one room.

Why it wins, in brief order: line 1 names a real moment through a sentence
said out loud on a named day, and line 2 names the irreversible change that
followed instead of restating the warning; the two lines work as one warning
and one consequence with no wasted words, in a plain voice written to one
person.
